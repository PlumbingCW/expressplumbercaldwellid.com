What water line repair covers

The water line, or service line, carries water from the meter to your house. A leak or break in it shows up as a wet yard, falling pressure, an unexplained rise in your bill or no water at all.

Repair may be a short dig to replace a failed section, a trenchless replacement to avoid tearing up a driveway, or a new line with a new shutoff valve. A leak detection visit comes first if the break is not obvious.

Water line leak: what to do first

Close the main valve where the line enters the house. If water is pooling in the yard, keep children and pets out of the soft ground, which can be unstable above a broken line.

The meter and the pipe up to it belong to the City of Caldwell, so do not touch the meter box. If water is coming from the meter itself, call the water department at 208-455-3070.

Signs your water line is failing

A wet or soft patch in the yard

Water seeping up from a buried line, often in a straight line toward the house.

Low water pressure everywhere

A leak before the house steals pressure from every fixture.

A spinning meter with everything off

Water is leaving through a leak, and the service line is a prime suspect.

Discolored or muddy water

A break can let soil into the line.

Sound of rushing water near the meter

A hiss near the entry point suggests a leak just before the house.

Sinkholes or sunken ground

Soil washing away over a break leaves a depression.

Pressure, valves and pressure reducers

High pressure strains every pipe in the house. A pressure regulator near the main, set within the normal home range, protects fixtures and appliances, and a failed one can lead to repeated leaks.

The plumber checks pressure with a gauge on a hose bib and replaces a worn regulator or a stuck main shutoff if needed. A working main valve is the single best defense when a pipe breaks, and it should be tested once a year.

Backflow assemblies and annual tests

Some properties have a backflow preventer on the water line, such as a lawn irrigation connection or a commercial service. The assembly keeps water that has been in the property from flowing back into the city supply, and it needs a test every year.

The owner pays for the test, and the City of Caldwell Water Department keeps a list of approved testers and takes questions at (208) 455-3070, option 2. If an assembly leaks or fails a test, a plumber can repair or replace it as part of the water line work.

Who owns what, and the call before you dig

The City of Caldwell's water ordinance makes the owner responsible for leaks in service pipes on the property side, and the meter is city property. The city water department, at 208-455-3070, can tell you where its responsibility ends if the break is near the meter.

Buried utilities should be located and marked before any excavation, and a plumber digging a service line handles that call. Idaho's plumbing bureau also notes that a permit is required whenever plumbing work is done, so ask who pulls it. Homeowners may permit work on their own primary or secondary residence.

A leak whose location is unknown starts with leak detection, and a split pipe inside the house is a burst pipe repair job, with the same crews serving Middleton addresses.

A new or replaced service line is built to the 2015 Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) with Idaho amendments, and the pipe and fittings should be installed by a plumber licensed in Idaho.
