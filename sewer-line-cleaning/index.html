What sewer line cleaning covers

Sewer line cleaning clears the main drain that carries waste from your house to the public sewer. The plumber opens the cleanout, runs a heavy cable or a jetting hose through the line and flushes the debris toward the street.

It is the right call for a main line that is slow, gurgling or fully blocked. If it keeps clogging, a camera shows why, and sewer line repair or a scheduled hydro jetting may be the next step.

Main sewer backup: what to do now

The lowest drain in the house is the first to show a main line problem. If water is coming up in a basement floor drain or a first-floor tub, stop every water use.

Do not flush, do not run the dishwasher and do not add drain cleaner. If your neighbors are affected too, report it to the City of Caldwell sewer emergency line at (208) 949-1278.

Cleanouts: where the plumber gets in

A cleanout is a capped pipe that gives direct access to the main line. In many homes it sits in a yard near the foundation, in a garage wall or in a basement floor, and homes without one require the plumber to pull a toilet.

Finding yours before a backup saves time. Keep the cap clear of soil and mulch, and tell the dispatcher where it is so the right access gear is brought.

Signs your main line needs cleaning

Slow drains everywhere

When every fixture drains slowly, the common line is narrowed.

Gurgling in the toilet

Air displaced by a restriction bubbles through the traps.

Water backs up in a tub when the toilet flushes

The two share a line, and the weakest trap shows the problem.

Sewer odor from a floor drain

Gas pushes out when the line is partly full.

Repeated clogs in older homes

Roots, grease or scale grow back in the same spots.

A wet spot near the cleanout

Backed up water can find the cleanout cap and seep out.

Storm drains and yard drains

Yard drains, driveway drains and storm inlets near the house clog with leaves, silt and trash. They are not part of the sewer, and they should never connect to it. The plumber clears the line and checks where it goes.

Grease and household chemicals should never go into storm drains or the street. The City of Caldwell's pretreatment program says so plainly. For repeat flooding at a drain, a camera shows whether the pipe is broken or crushed.

The Caldwell public sewer behind your line

The City of Caldwell's Wastewater Treatment Plant, a division of Public Works, runs the public sewer system, which includes more than 300 miles of sewer lines, 4,970 manholes and 16 lift stations. Its sewer emergency line is (208) 949-1278. If the line is clear to the street and still backs up, the problem may be in the public main.

The city's pretreatment program reminds residents not to pour cooking oil or grease into sinks, garbage disposals, toilets or floor drains. Grease cools in the pipe and builds the same coating that sends so many main lines back for cleaning.

Cleanout work, and any pipe replacement that follows it, is governed by the 2015 Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) with Idaho amendments, the state code listed in IDAPA 24.39.20.
