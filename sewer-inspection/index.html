A sewer camera inspection shows what is happening inside a pipe you cannot dig up. A plumber runs a small camera through the cleanout and watches a live feed for roots, cracks, sags and blockages, so repairs are based on what is really there.

What a sewer camera inspection shows

A sewer camera is a waterproof lens on a flexible cable, pushed through a cleanout with a light and a locating transmitter. The plumber watches the feed on a monitor and notes the pipe material, joints, roots, cracks and any places where water sits.

With a locator, the plumber can mark the surface above a problem and measure how deep it is and how far from the cleanout. Those facts turn a vague plumbing problem into a specific repair quote.

Buying a home in Caldwell

Roughly 29 percent of Caldwell's housing units were built before 1980, and the median home dates from 2003, according to Census Bureau estimates for 2020 to 2024. Older lines may be cast iron or clay, and newer ones are usually PVC.

A home inspection rarely goes below the surface. A sewer camera run before closing is the way to learn about a cracked line or a root mass, and the video gives you something concrete to bring to the seller.

Why cross-bores deserve a look

A cross-bore happens when a gas, water or fiber line is installed by trenchless boring and passes through a sewer pipe. The line may stay hidden until someone cuts a cable through it.

In January 2026 a contractor's cross-bore through a city sewer main at Denton Street and Ashton Avenue in Caldwell caused a blockage and a small overflow, as reported by local news. If boring crews have been working near your home, a camera inspection can confirm your lateral is clear.

What the camera cannot do

A camera cannot see through standing water, and it cannot always pass a collapsed section. The plumber may clean the line first so the lens can see the wall. A camera also shows the pipe at one moment, not what happens in a storm or a freeze.

If the camera finds damage, the next step is a repair quote. See sewer line repair for the options, and drain cleaning if the line only needs clearing.

Where your lateral meets the public main

The City of Caldwell operates the public sewer system through its Wastewater Treatment Plant, a division of Public Works. The plant lists more than 300 miles of sewer lines, 4,970 manholes and 16 lift stations. A camera cannot inspect the city main from your cleanout, but it can tell the plumber whether the problem is on your side.

Idaho's Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses says a permit is required when plumbing work is performed. If the camera finds a defect that needs repair, ask the plumber who pulls the permit.
