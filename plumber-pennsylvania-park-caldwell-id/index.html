Where it is and who built it

A listing guide describes Pennsylvania Park as near downtown Caldwell. Its directions run from Interstate 84 east on Highway 20/26, south on Smeed Parkway and east on Enterprise. Delaware Park is to the east and Pheasant Run to the south.

The builder is CBH Homes, and the guide says the subdivision was developed in 2018 with homes built between 2018 and 2020. Lot sizes are given as about 0.14 to 0.25 acre, though one section of the same page says 0.17 to 0.25. There is a Pennsylvania Park HOA, and the schools listed are East Canyon Elementary, Vallivue Middle and Ridgevue High.

Houses that are a few years in

The guide gives build years of 2018 to 2020, which makes those homes about six to eight years old, although it also lists new-home sales through 2023. At that age the original builder-grade faucets, supply lines and toilet fill valves begin to show wear. None of that is unusual, and none of it is a claim about this subdivision in particular.

Check what your warranty still covers before hiring someone else. After that, a faucet repair, a toilet repair or a garbage disposal repair is usually a parts-and-labor visit rather than a large job.

Testing your tap before buying a softener

The city has not published a hardness figure that this page could confirm. A water softener is a reasonable response to scale, but the sizing depends on a real number from your own water.

Test first, then choose the equipment. A softener needs a drain connection and a power outlet near the water line, and the plumber should explain where the salt goes and how the unit drains before anything is installed.

Pennsylvania Park and the city's services

The City of Caldwell data hub lists the City Water Department for water and the City Wastewater Treatment Plant for sewer, with Intermountain Gas and Idaho Power for fuel and power. Your own bill shows who serves your address.

The CBH listing page names a Pennsylvania Park HOA. HOA rules about exterior equipment, such as where a softener or a tankless unit may go, are worth reading before work. Idaho requires a permit when plumbing work is performed, according to the Idaho DOPL Plumbing Bureau FAQ. Ask whoever does the work who pulls the permit and when the inspection is booked.
