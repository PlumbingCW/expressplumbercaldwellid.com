What faucet and sink repair covers

Faucets fail at the cartridge, the washer, the O-rings or the spout, and each fault has a typical symptom. A drip from the spout points to the cartridge or seat, while a wet base points to O-rings, and low flow points to a clogged aerator or a failing supply valve.

Under the sink, the supply lines and shutoff valves wear out on their own schedule. A braided line can burst, and a valve that has not turned in years may break when you try it, which is why the plumber replaces old valves during a fixture visit.

Water flooding the cabinet: first steps

Turn the oval or round handles on the two small valves under the sink clockwise. If they are stuck or leak when turned, shut off the main valve at the house.

Clear the cabinet, soak up water with towels and open the doors to dry the space. If water has reached an outlet or disposal wiring, stay clear and leave the power off.

Signs a faucet or sink needs attention

A steady drip from the spout

A worn cartridge or seat is letting water past.

Water pooling at the base of the faucet

O-rings or seals have failed.

Low flow from one tap only

The aerator is clogged or the supply valve is partly closed.

A handle that is hard to turn

Mineral buildup or a worn cartridge.

Moisture in the cabinet under the sink

A supply line, drain connection or valve is leaking.

A slow drain that gurgles

The sink trap or branch drain is partly blocked. See drain cleaning.

  • Parts are discontinued

Supply valves and braided lines

The two valves under a sink, called angle stops, are what let you shut water off for one fixture. They often seize from mineral buildup, and a plumber replaces them when they leak or will not close.

Braided stainless supply lines are common, and rubber washers inside them wear out over years. If a line is bulging or rusty at the ends, it is due, and replacing lines is a low-cost way to prevent a cabinet flood.
