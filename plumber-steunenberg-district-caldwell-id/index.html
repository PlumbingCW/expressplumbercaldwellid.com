Plumber in the Steunenberg District, Caldwell, ID

The Steunenberg Residential Historic District is the older neighborhood beside The College of Idaho, set aside by the City of Caldwell to protect its homes. This page covers plumbing help for houses in and around it, and what a historic address does and does not change.

Where the district sits

The city describes the district as running along The College of Idaho at 20th Avenue, Blaine Street and Fillmore Street, which continues down to 16th Avenue South. From there the edge follows Everett Street up to 12th Avenue South and returns to Blaine Street. One block of East Cleveland, between 12th and 13th Avenues South, is excluded.

The National Register listing, called the Caldwell Residential Historic District, is described as roughly bounded by Cleveland Boulevard, Everett Street, South 12th Avenue and South 20th Avenue. The two descriptions are close but not identical, so if your address is near an edge, ask the city's Planning and Zoning Department whether it is inside.

What the record says about the houses

The city says the district holds more than 330 residential properties and calls it one of the most historically intact neighborhoods in Idaho. A 2018 article in Territory magazine put the count at more than 300 and said most houses date from the 1910s to the 1930s, in styles from Queen Anne to Tudor Revival.

The National Register listing names Bungalow and Craftsman and Tudor Revival among the styles. None of these sources says what pipes, drains or water heaters are in any one house. That is the part a plumber has to look at, and it is why the sections below separate what is known from what is not.

Plumbing in a house this old: look first, then decide

A house built a century ago has usually had work done since. Some pipes may have been replaced, some patched and some left alone. Guessing which is which is how people end up replacing lines that were fine.

The practical order is to look first. A sewer camera inspection shows the drain line from the cleanout to the street. Leak detection finds a hidden leak when a water bill climbs for no clear reason. If the lines are failing, repiping and water line repair are the next conversation, with the findings in hand.

Historic review and plumbing work

The city says a Certificate of Appropriateness is required for exterior changes that can be seen from the street or public right of way. Applications go through Planning and Zoning, the Historic Preservation Commission hears them at a monthly public meeting, and its decision can be appealed. The city notes the decision serves as a recommendation to the building permit process.

The city page speaks about exteriors. It does not say how interior plumbing is treated. If a job would change something visible from the street, such as a roof vent, outdoor equipment or a relocated meter box, ask Planning and Zoning before work starts. Idaho requires a permit when plumbing work is performed, according to the Idaho DOPL Plumbing Bureau FAQ. Ask whoever does the work who pulls the permit and when the inspection is booked.

Who serves Steunenberg District addresses

The City of Caldwell data hub lists the Caldwell City Water Department for water and the Caldwell City Wastewater Treatment Plant for sewer. It lists Intermountain Gas for natural gas and Idaho Power for electricity.

Which of those serve one particular address is on your own bills, so check there first. A leak or blockage inside the house is the owner's to fix, while a problem at the street or the main is usually the utility's.

Steunenberg District plumbing questions

The city page covers exterior changes, not who may do plumbing. Idaho licenses plumbers and requires plumbing work to be permitted, so ask for the license number and check it on the Idaho DOPL license search.

A like-for-like job inside the house is not an exterior change. If the work would alter something visible from the street, ask Planning and Zoning first. Idaho requires a permit when plumbing work is performed, according to the Idaho DOPL Plumbing Bureau FAQ. Ask whoever does the work who pulls the permit and when the inspection is booked.
