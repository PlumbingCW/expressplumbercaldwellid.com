What burst pipe repair covers

Burst pipe repair stops the water, finds the split section and replaces it. The plumber cuts out the damaged length and splices in new pipe, whether the line is copper, PEX or older galvanized steel.

If one pipe has burst from age or freezing, the plumber also checks nearby runs. Several failures in a short time can point to a larger problem and a conversation about repiping.

What to do first when a pipe bursts

Close the main shutoff valve, which is commonly near the water heater, in a garage or at the front of the house. Then open the lowest faucet to drain remaining water.

Turn off the water heater to protect the tank. If the water is near outlets, appliances or the electrical panel, stay out of the area and shut off power only where you can reach the breaker dry. Move valuables, photograph the damage and call.

Signs a pipe has frozen or is about to burst

Little or no water from a tap in freezing weather

Ice is blocking the line, and the pipe may split as it thaws.

Frost on a visible pipe

A white coating on a pipe in a garage or crawl space shows it is freezing.

A bulge or crack in a pipe

The pipe is stressed and about to fail.

Water stains on a wall in cold weather

A split behind the drywall may be leaking slowly.

The sound of running water with taps off

A split pipe can hiss or drip inside walls.

A sudden drop in water pressure

A large break can drain pressure from the whole house.

Why pipes burst and when it is not freezing

Frozen pipe burst

  • Pipe walls in unheated space
  • Ice expands and splits the pipe
  • Often at the freeze point, not at the thaw
  • Prevent with insulation and heat

Pipe burst from age or pressure

  • Corrosion thins the wall
  • High pressure stresses weak joints
  • Can happen in any season
  • Prevent with a pressure check and repiping

Winter weather in the Treasure Valley

NOAA climate normals for the Nampa Sugar Factory station, about 7 miles east, show a January average low near 23 degrees F and about 132 days a year at or below freezing. Pipes in garages, crawl spaces, exterior walls and attics are the usual casualties.

To protect them, disconnect garden hoses, open cabinet doors on exterior walls during hard freezes and let a faucet drip slowly. If a pipe freezes but has not burst, thaw it slowly with a hair dryer and keep the faucet open until water runs.

Repair choices after a break

A burst section is cut out and replaced with copper or PEX, joined with fittings that match the existing line. PEX tolerates freezing better than rigid pipe because it can expand, which makes it a common choice when a run is replaced.

If the burst is in galvanized or old copper pipe, the plumber may recommend replacing the whole run now to avoid a second failure. See water line repair when the break is outside the house.
