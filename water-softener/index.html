What a water softener does

A softener swaps calcium and magnesium, the minerals that make water hard, for sodium or potassium as water passes through a resin bed. Over time the resin loads up, and the unit flushes itself with brine to recharge.

Hard water leaves scale in water heaters, fixtures and pipes and shortens appliance life. A softener helps with all of those, but it is not a drinking water filter and does not remove most contaminants.

Test before you buy

Hardness varies from tap to tap and from well to well, and published water reports do not always list it. The City of Caldwell's water department can say what it reports for your area, and a low-cost test kit or lab test gives you a number for your own tap.

Sizing a softener to a number is far better than guessing. A unit that is too small regenerates constantly, and one that is too large wastes salt and water.

Where it goes and what it connects to

A softener is installed on the main water line after the shutoff and before the water heater, with a bypass so the house can run on untreated water during service. It needs a drain for regeneration water, an air gap and a power outlet.

Outdoor taps are usually left on untreated water to save salt. The plumber also checks that the drain connection meets code, which ties to the plumbing installation work around the unit.

Taking care of a softener

Keep the brine tank at least a third full of the right salt, and check for salt bridges that block the cycle. Once or twice a year, the plumber can sanitize the resin bed, check settings and inspect the bypass valve.

A softener can also help protect a water heater from scale. See water heater repair for what scale does to a tank and element.

Caldwell water and local rules

The City of Caldwell water department supplies drinking water from groundwater wells and publishes annual water quality reports. Not every report lists hardness, so a test at your own tap is the best way to get a number. The department is at 208-455-3070.

Some Caldwell homes are on pressurized irrigation run by the Caldwell Municipal Irrigation District, which is a separate system from drinking water. A softener belongs on the drinking water line, not on the irrigation supply. Idaho requires a permit for plumbing work, so ask the plumber who pulls it.

Softeners often follow a water heater installation, and the crews work in Middleton as well.
