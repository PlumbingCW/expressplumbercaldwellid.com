What commercial plumbing covers

Commercial plumbing handles buildings with more fixtures, bigger drains and tougher rules than a house. It covers floor drains, mop sinks, restroom groups, kitchen lines, water heaters, backflow devices and grease interceptors.

A shop owner or property manager needs fast diagnosis and a written price. The plumber works around business hours where possible and keeps the dispatcher informed so you can plan service interruptions. Drain clearing often uses hydro jetting when lines carry grease.

Business plumbing emergency: first steps

Shut off the main valve for a flood, and stop using fixtures that drain slowly or back up. Put out wet-floor signs and keep customers and staff clear of wet areas.

If sewage is coming up, close the affected restrooms or the kitchen, and call the 24/7 line. For a gas smell, leave and call 911 and your gas utility first.

Grease interceptors and kitchen drains

The City of Caldwell's pretreatment program, run by its Pretreatment Department at 208-477-7953, governs fats, oil and grease from food businesses. It requires interceptors to be designed and installed by a licensed plumber and asks businesses to keep cleaning records.

The program also says no grease should go into sinks, disposals, toilets or floor drains, and it warns against additives that claim to dissolve grease. A plumber can size an interceptor, set up a cleaning schedule and keep a log you can show an inspector.

Signs a commercial system needs service

Slow kitchen drains at the end of the day

Grease has coated the line, and the interceptor may be full.

Sewer odor in a restaurant

A full interceptor or a dry floor drain trap lets gas into the room.

Floor drains that back up

The main line is partly blocked.

Water heater that cannot keep up

Demand has outgrown the tank. See water heater replacement.

Restrooms that flush weakly

A shared branch line is clogged or undersized.

A high water bill with no change in business

A leak, a running toilet or a stuck valve.

Backflow, permits and records

Commercial water systems often have backflow assemblies that must be tested every year at the owner's expense. The City of Caldwell keeps a list of approved testers, and the water department can be reached at 208-455-3070, option 2, or backflow@cityofcaldwell.org.

Larger changes need a permit. Idaho states that a permit is required when plumbing work is performed, so ask who pulls it and keep the inspection record with the building files.

Caldwell businesses, grease rules and the public sewer

Caldwell has a busy downtown around Indian Creek Plaza, where the city hosts more than 300 events a year, and a wide mix of restaurants, shops and offices. Food businesses in that mix are the ones the pretreatment program is aimed at.

The City of Caldwell's sewer system includes more than 300 miles of line, 4,970 manholes and 16 lift stations, and a sewer emergency line at (208) 949-1278. A backup that affects several neighboring businesses may be in the public main, and reporting it helps the city respond.

Shops and offices in Middleton use the same dispatch line, and a failing water heater at a business is handled under water heater repair.

Idaho's adopted plumbing code, the 2015 Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) with Idaho amendments, applies to commercial buildings as well as homes.
