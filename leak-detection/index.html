What leak detection involves

Leak detection finds where water is escaping before anything is opened up. The plumber starts with a pressure check and a meter test, then narrows the search with listening gear, thermal imaging and tracer methods that fit the pipe and the location.

The goal is a small, exact repair instead of a large demolition. Once the leak is found, the repair is quoted before work starts, and larger jobs such as a buried service line move to water line repair.

Active leak: what to do now

Find the main shutoff, usually near where the water line enters the house, and turn it off. Open a faucet at the lowest point to drain the pressure.

If water has reached outlets, light fixtures or appliances, stay off wet floors and shut off the breaker only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. Photograph the damage for insurance and call.

The meter test you can do yourself

Turn off every fixture and appliance, then look at the water meter. If the small leak indicator spins or the numbers move with nothing running, water is escaping somewhere on your side of the meter.

Write down the reading, wait an hour with all water off and check again. If the number changed, the leak is real, and the next question is whether it is inside the house, under the slab or in the yard.

Signs of a hidden leak

Slab, wall and ceiling leaks

Slab leaks are among the hardest to catch because the pipe is buried in concrete. A warm floor spot, a hissing sound or a bill spike are the usual clues, and the plumber confirms the leak with a pressure test before locating it.

Ceiling and wall stains are tricky because water travels. A stain in one room can start in a bathroom or attic far from the visible mark, so the plumber traces the path with a moisture meter and a thermal camera rather than cutting the first stained spot.

Irrigation, pool and yard lines

Many Caldwell properties have a separate irrigation system, and some are on the pressurized irrigation service run by the Caldwell Municipal Irrigation District rather than city drinking water. A leaking valve or a frost-cracked pipe in that system wastes water and floods the lawn.

The plumber isolates each zone and tests the pressure to find the line at fault. A pool plumbing leak is tested the same way, with the pool lines isolated one at a time.

Rental, multifamily and commercial buildings

Property managers often call about a building where the meter keeps running but no tenant reports a leak. In a multifamily or commercial building the search starts by isolating each unit or branch, then testing the underground lines between them, which is faster than opening walls unit by unit.

Fire lines and large service lines need locating gear that suits bigger pipe, so say what the building has when you call. The business side of the work, including grease and backflow, is covered under commercial plumbing.

Leaks, your water bill and the city meter

The City of Caldwell's water ordinance makes the owner responsible for leaks in service pipes and fixtures on the property side, and says wasteful leaks should be fixed within 60 days of being notified by the city. Meters are city property.

The city's utility billing policy says it does not adjust a bill for excess water from a leak, with narrow exceptions such as a line break or failed parts on the city side of the meter. If you believe an exception applies, utility billing is at (208) 455-3000, option 1. Finding and fixing a leak early is the best protection for your bill.

Once the leak is found, a split pipe is repaired as described under burst pipe repair, and plumbers make the same visits in Middleton.

Finding a leak is not code work, but the repair that follows is, and Idaho applies the 2015 Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) with its own amendments to it.
