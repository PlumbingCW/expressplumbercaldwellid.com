Before we arrive

A few quick steps can limit damage while you wait for the plumber. None of them require tools.

Find the main valve

Turn it clockwise to stop all water, and open a low faucet to drain the pressure.

Protect the electrics

Keep clear of outlets and the panel if the floor is wet, and shut off breakers only where you can reach them dry.

Stop using drains

If a drain is backing up, no flushing, laundry or dishwasher until the plumber clears the line.

Clear a path

Move items from around the water heater, under sinks or near the cleanout so the plumber can start right away.

Why Middleton plumbing is mostly newer plumbing

The Census Bureau's 2020 to 2024 estimates give Middleton a median year built of 2009, with about 71 percent of homes built in 2000 or later and about 90 percent owner-occupied. The city's population is estimated at about 12,600 in 2025, up from 9,425 in the 2020 Census.

For homeowners, that means fewer galvanized pipes and more PEX or copper, but also newer water heaters in garages, irrigation systems tied to the supply and long, shallow drain runs on slab foundations. A water heater at ten or twelve years old is the usual failure here, so replacement is a common call.

Middleton water, public works and building permits

The City of Middleton supplies drinking water from seven wells, according to its 2023 water quality report, and the report does not list a hardness figure. If you want a number, test your tap or ask Public Works at (208) 585-3133.

The Middleton Building Department shares an office with Public Works at 1103 W Main St. Idaho states that a permit is required when plumbing work is performed, so ask the plumber who pulls it and when the inspection is booked.
