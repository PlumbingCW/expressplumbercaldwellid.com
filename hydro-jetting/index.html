What hydro jetting does that a cable cannot

Hydro jetting sends water at very high pressure through a hose with a nozzle that fires jets forward and backward. The spray scours grease, scale, soap and silt off the inside of the pipe, and flushes the debris toward the street connection.

A cable pokes a hole through a blockage. A jet cleans the circle. In lines that clog again and again, the jet is the more lasting fix, and the camera that follows shows whether the pipe is clear and whole.

Cable and jet compared

Cable (auger)

  • Pokes through a single blockage
  • Fast and low cost for simple clogs
  • Leaves a coating on the pipe wall
  • Best for toilets, sinks and tubs

Hydro jet

  • Scours the full pipe wall
  • Clears grease, scale and fine roots
  • Needs access through a cleanout
  • Best for mains and repeat clogs

Main line backing up: what to do first

A main line backup comes up in the lowest fixtures first, usually a basement floor drain, a shower or a tub. Stop all water use, including the washer, and call.

Do not pour drain cleaner into a full line, since the plumber will have to work around it. If the line serves a small business, do not run the dishwasher or mop sink until the line is clear.

Signs that point to a jet, not a cable

A clog that returns in weeks

Grease or scale left on the wall regrows into a new blockage.

A cable that stalls at one spot

Restaurant or shop with heavy grease

An older iron or clay line

When a jet is the wrong tool

Jetting is not for pipe that is cracked, collapsed or badly separated. High pressure water can enlarge a break or push soil into the line. That is why a camera look comes first on old or suspect lines, and why the plumber may suggest a cable and a camera inspection before a jet.

A jet also works only where the nozzle can travel. A line with a belly, a hard offset or a missing section may need sewer line repair before it can be cleaned.

Grease rules and the city sewer system

The City of Caldwell's pretreatment program tells restaurants and other food businesses to have grease interceptors designed and installed by a licensed plumber, to keep cleaning records ready for inspection and to avoid additives that claim to dissolve grease. Scheduled jetting of a kitchen line is a common companion to interceptor service.

The city sewer system has more than 300 miles of main and 4,970 manholes, and its sewer emergency line is (208) 949-1278. A backup that spreads to neighboring homes may be in a public line and is worth reporting.

Restaurants, rentals and older homes

A restaurant kitchen line sees grease every day and may need jetting on a schedule, while a rental house with repeat clogs may need a jet and a camera check at tenant turnover. Property managers can call the same line to arrange work for several units.

Older homes often have cast iron or clay drains with rough walls that catch debris. A jet cleans them, and a camera tells you whether a repair is next. See commercial plumbing for kitchen drain work.
