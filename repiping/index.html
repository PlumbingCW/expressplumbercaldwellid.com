Repiping Services in Caldwell, ID

Repiping replaces worn supply lines throughout a house, ending the cycle of patching one leak after another. A plumber plans the route, protects your finishes and replaces the old pipe with new PEX or copper.

  • PEX and copper

What repiping means

Repiping swaps the hot and cold water supply lines inside a house for new pipe. It does not touch the drain lines, which carry waste away, and it does not replace the water line from the meter, which is covered under water line repair.

It can be done for the whole house or for part of it, such as a bathroom group. The plumber runs new lines through walls, attics and crawl spaces, connects every fixture and removes the old pipe or caps it.

Signs it may be time to repipe

Repeated pinhole leaks

When one patch is followed by another, the pipe wall is thinning.

Brown or rusty water

Galvanized steel corrodes from inside and sheds rust.

Low pressure at many fixtures

Mineral buildup and rust narrow the pipe.

Visible green or white crust

Corrosion at joints signals aging.

Flaking or bulging pipes

The pipe is nearing failure.

A recalled or problem pipe material

Some materials from certain decades have known defect histories.

PEX and copper compared

PEX

  • Flexible and quick to install
  • Resists freeze splitting
  • Lower material cost
  • Not for sunlight or outdoor use

Copper

  • Rigid, long track record
  • Higher material cost
  • Joints are soldered or pressed
  • Can corrode in aggressive water

Caldwell homes and when repiping is likely

The Census Bureau's 2020 to 2024 estimates show 22,378 housing units in Caldwell, with about 56 percent built in 2000 or later and about 29 percent built before 1980. The median year built is 2003.

That split means two different conversations. Homes built before 1980 may have galvanized steel or older copper that is reaching the end of its life, while newer homes more often need a spot repair, a pressure regulator or a manifold fix than a full repipe.

Permits, code and your options as an owner

Idaho's Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses says a permit is required when plumbing work is performed, and that homeowners may permit work on their own primary or secondary residence. The state plumbing code is the 2015 Uniform Plumbing Code with Idaho amendments, and permits expire after 365 days.

The City of Caldwell's Building Safety Division takes plumbing permit applications through its online portal and schedules inspections. Same-day inspection requests are taken by 7:00 a.m., so ask the plumber when the inspection will be booked.

Before committing to a repipe, a leak detection visit can show whether the trouble is one failing run or the whole system, and homes in Middleton are served the same way.

Repiping follows the 2015 Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) with Idaho amendments, and the finished system is pressure tested and inspected.

Source: Idaho DOPL Plumbing Bureau FAQ and the City of Caldwell Building Safety Division

Living in the house during a repipe

A whole-house repipe commonly takes a few days, depending on the size of the house. Water is shut off for part of each day, and the plumber tells you when. Plan to have drinking water and a place to wash up for those hours.

Furniture along exterior walls and under attic hatches should be moved before the visit. The plumber protects floors and cleans up each day.
