Clogged Toilet Repair for Caldwell, ID Homes

A clogged toilet is usually a blockage in the trap just below the bowl, and a flange plunger clears most of them in a few minutes. If water rises toward the rim, stop flushing, lift the tank lid and close the flapper so the bowl does not overflow.

When plunging fails or other drains start to gurgle, the clog is deeper. This guide explains how to tell the difference.

If the bowl is about to overflow

Take the tank lid off and push the flapper down, or turn the supply valve behind the toilet clockwise. Do not flush again, because every flush adds more water to a bowl that cannot drain.

Lay towels at the base and keep children away from the area. If overflow reaches a hallway or other fixtures start to back up, stop all water use and call.

What clogs a toilet

Too much paper is the most common cause. Wipes, even ones labeled flushable, do not break down in the line, and toys, cotton swabs and feminine products stick in the trap or the drain beyond it.

Low flush power is another cause. An older toilet may not push waste through the trap in one flush, and a weak flush can leave residue that builds into a clog over weeks.

When the toilet is not the problem

If a toilet clogs and the tub or sink gurgles at the same time, the blockage is in the branch or main drain. A plunger will not fix that, and the right service is drain cleaning with a cable or a jet.

Frequent clogs in an older house can mean roots or a belly in the drain. A camera inspection shows what is happening. See sewer camera inspection.

Caldwell sewers and what not to flush

The City of Caldwell's Wastewater Treatment Plant, a division of Public Works, runs a sewer system with more than 300 miles of line and 16 lift stations. Wipes and other items that do not break down can clog both the line from your house and the city system.

Its pretreatment program asks residents to keep cooking oil and grease out of toilets, sinks and floor drains. If a backup affects your neighbors too, the city sewer emergency line is (208) 949-1278.

Setting a new toilet or replacing a flange falls under Idaho's adopted 2015 Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), so leave anything beyond a plunger or a flapper to a licensed plumber.

How to plunge a toilet properly

1

Use the right plunger

A flange plunger has a rubber extension that seals the drain opening, and a flat cup plunger does not.

2

Cover the cup

Make sure the bowl has enough water to cover the cup, and add water if it is low.

3

Push gently first

The first push should be slow to push out the air, then pump firmly ten or fifteen times.

4

Pull to finish

The pull stroke does the most work, so finish with a sharp pull and watch the water drop.

What a plumber can do that a plunger cannot

A plumber has a closet auger that reaches past the trap, a camera for the drain line and the tools to pull the toilet and clear it from below. Pulling a toilet also lets the plumber inspect the flange and replace the wax ring, which matters if the base has been leaking.

If the clog came from a toy or other hard object, pulling the toilet is often the cleanest way to get it out without cracking the porcelain. The visit ends with a flush test and a check that the seal at the base is dry.

Rentals, guests and busy households

Landlords and hosts see more toilet clogs because guests do not know the quirks of an older fixture. A short note in the bathroom about what not to flush prevents most service calls.

If a rental toilet clogs repeatedly, compare the cost of repeat visits against replacing the toilet. Newer models flush with less water and clear waste better, and a single replacement can end the pattern.

Prevent the next clog

Use only toilet paper and human waste, and keep a small trash can with a lid by the toilet for everything else. Teach children early, because toys are a common cause.

If the toilet is an older model with weak flushing, a replacement is worth discussing. Roughly 29 percent of Caldwell homes were built before 1980, and many of those have a second or third generation toilet that still wastes water.
