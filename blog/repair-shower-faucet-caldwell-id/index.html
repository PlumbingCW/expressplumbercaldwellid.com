How to Repair a Shower Faucet in a Caldwell, ID Home

A dripping or stiff shower faucet is almost always a worn cartridge, and swapping it takes under an hour once the water is off. First close the shutoff for the bathroom or the main valve, and cover the drain so no screws fall in.

This guide covers the common faults, what a repair involves and when the job is bigger than a cartridge.

Start with the symptom

A drip from the showerhead after you turn the water off points to the cartridge or the valve seat. Water that stays lukewarm when you set the handle to hot points to a worn mixing cartridge or a failed anti-scald stop.

Low flow from one showerhead usually means mineral buildup in the holes, which a vinegar soak can clear. Low flow at every fixture is a different problem and may point to a pressure issue.

What a cartridge replacement involves

1

Shut off the water

Close the bathroom shutoff or the main valve and open the tub tap to drain.

2

Remove the handle

A small screw hides under a cap, and the trim plate comes off next.

3

Pull the cartridge

A retaining clip or nut holds it, and some need a puller.

4

Install and test

A new cartridge goes in the same way, then the water is turned on slowly.

When it is more than a cartridge

If the valve body is cracked, the stops are corroded or there is a leak behind the wall, a cartridge swap will not fix it. The plumber opens the wall from the back side or through a small access, replaces the valve and patches the opening.

An old shower valve that has never been serviced can also fail to shut off at all. That is the moment to replace the main shutoff, because a stuck stop can turn a small leak into a flood. Hidden wall leaks are found with leak detection.

Low flow and showerhead buildup

If the shower flow is weak but the cartridge works, unscrew the showerhead and soak it in white vinegar for an hour. Mineral deposits block the small holes, and a toothpick or soft brush frees them without scratching the finish.

A flow restrictor behind the head limits water use and can be removed, but check local rules and consider your water bill first. If every tap in the house is low, the issue is likely upstream, such as a partly closed main valve or a failing pressure regulator.

Keeping the water at a safe temperature

Shower valves with a pressure balancing or thermostatic cartridge keep the temperature steady when someone flushes a toilet. If the water swings hot and cold, the cartridge is worn and should be replaced soon.

Water heaters are also commonly set too high. A setting near 120 degrees F lowers scald risk, particularly for children and older adults, and also reduces scale and energy use.

Caldwell water bills and old shower valves

The City of Caldwell's billing policy says it does not adjust a bill for excess water from a leak on the owner's side, so a dripping shower can show up on the statement. Utility billing can be reached at (208) 455-3000, option 1.

The Census Bureau's 2020 to 2024 estimates show about 29 percent of Caldwell homes were built before 1980. Original shower valves in those houses are decades old and often due for replacement during any bathroom visit.

Opening a wall to replace a shower valve is plumbing work under Idaho's adopted 2015 Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

Source: City of Caldwell Utility Billing and U.S. Census Bureau ACS 2020 to 2024

  • ✓A towel or plug to cover the drain
  • ✓The brand and model number of the valve, or a photo of the old cartridge

When to call a plumber instead

Turning off the water and swapping a cartridge is routine, but opening walls or moving pipes is plumbing work that needs a permit under Idaho rules. The Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses states that a permit is required when plumbing work is performed.

Ask any plumber who pulls the permit on a larger job. See plumbing installation for new fixtures and remodels.
