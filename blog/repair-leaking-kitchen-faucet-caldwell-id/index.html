How to Repair a Leaking Kitchen Faucet in Caldwell, ID

A leaking kitchen faucet drips from one of three places: the spout, the base of the handle or the cabinet underneath. Dry the sink, run the water and watch where the first drop appears, since each location has a different fix.

Close the small valves under the sink before you take anything apart, and put a towel over the drain.

Fixing a spout or handle drip

Most single handle faucets use a cartridge that wears out after years of use. Remove the handle, lift out the cartridge and bring it to a supplier or pick the matching part from the manufacturer's guide.

O-rings around the swivel spout often fail before the cartridge does. A dab of plumber's grease on new O-rings usually stops a leak at the base for years.

Water under the sink: do this first

Close the two angle stop valves under the sink by turning the handles clockwise. If they will not close or leak when turned, close the main water valve for the house.

Clear the cabinet, mop the floor and dry the cabinet floor. Damp particleboard swells and can mold, so open the doors and run a fan if you can.

The pieces under the sink

The braided supply lines and the angle stops are the parts that fail quietly. A line with a rusty collar or a bulge is due, and replacing both lines and both stops is a small job that prevents a cabinet flood.

If the sink also has a disposal, check its mounting and drain connections, because a leak there looks like a faucet leak. See garbage disposal repair when that is the source.

Caldwell billing, hard water and old fixtures

The City of Caldwell's billing policy says it does not adjust bills for excess water from leaks on the owner's side, so a faucet that drips all month shows up in the bill. Utility billing is at (208) 455-3000, option 1.

The city draws its water from groundwater wells, and published reports do not always list hardness. Mineral scale shortens the life of cartridges and aerators, so test your tap if you notice crust at the spout.

Replacing supply lines and shutoff valves is plumbing work under Idaho's adopted 2015 Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

Low flow from the spout

A kitchen faucet with weak flow often has a clogged aerator, which is the small screen at the tip of the spout. Unscrew it, rinse out the debris and soak it in vinegar before putting it back.

If the flow is still low, the supply line or the angle stop may be partly closed or full of debris. Try opening the valve fully and flush the line into a bucket before reattaching the faucet.

Pull-down sprayers and hoses

Pull-down and pull-out faucets use a hose that runs under the sink with a plastic weight to pull it back. A leak that appears only when the sprayer is used points to a cracked hose or a loose quick-connect fitting.

The fix is usually a new hose, which snaps on in a few minutes. If the weight has slipped, the sprayer will not retract and the head may hang from the spout, so reposition it on the hose before it wears a hole.

When to replace the faucet instead

A faucet with a corroded body, a pitted finish or discontinued parts is cheaper to replace than to chase. A new faucet with new supply lines also resets the clock on the parts most likely to fail.
