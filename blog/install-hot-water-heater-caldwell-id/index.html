Installing a Hot Water Heater in Caldwell, ID

Installing a hot water heater in Caldwell takes most of a day. A licensed plumber removes the old tank, sets the new one, connects water and fuel, adds the safety valves and has the work inspected under an Idaho plumbing permit.

This guide covers how to choose the fuel and size, what the permit involves and what to have ready before the plumber arrives.

What install day looks like

1

Drain and disconnect

The old tank is shut off, drained and unhooked from the water and fuel lines.

2

Set the new unit

The tank goes in a drain pan with fresh connectors and a pressure relief valve.

3

Connect fuel and vent

Gas lines, venting or electrical connections are made and leak tested.

4

Fill, test and inspect

The tank is filled, fired and checked, then the inspection is booked.

Choosing between gas, electric and heat pump

The easiest swap is the same fuel as the old tank, because the gas line, vent or electrical circuit is already in place. Changing fuel is possible but adds work, such as a new circuit, a vent or a condensate drain.

A heat pump water heater uses electricity to move heat from the surrounding air and needs open space around it. It works best in a garage or utility room that stays above freezing, so consider the winter lows described below before choosing one.

Sizing the tank for the household

A common rule of thumb is about 40 gallons for one or two people, 50 gallons for three or four and larger tanks for bigger households. The first hour rating on the label says how much hot water the unit delivers in a busy hour, and it matters as much as tank size.

Tell the plumber how many bathrooms you run at once and whether you have a soaking tub or a dishwasher with a hot water inlet. Those details decide whether a standard tank is enough or a larger unit is worth the extra cost.

Caldwell homes, cold garages and permits

The Census Bureau's 2020 to 2024 estimates give Caldwell a median year built of 2003, with about 56 percent of homes built in 2000 or later. A tank installed when the house was new is now more than 20 years old, well past the 8 to 12 years most tanks last, so a failing original unit is a common reason for calls.

NOAA normals for the Nampa station, about 7 miles east, show a January average low near 23 degrees F and about 132 days a year at or below freezing. Water heaters in unheated garages lose heat faster in that weather, and an insulated tank or a location change can help.

Idaho's Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses says a permit is required when plumbing work is performed. The City of Caldwell Building Safety Division handles plumbing permits through its online portal, and same-day inspection requests are taken by 7:00 a.m.

What to do before the plumber arrives

Clear a path to the tank and move stored items back a few feet. Know where your main water valve and the tank's shutoff are, and tell the plumber about any past leaks or odd noises from the old unit.

If you smell gas at any point, leave the building, then call 911 and your gas utility from outside. A plumber can finish the job once the utility says it is safe. For a failed tank that cannot wait, see water heater replacement.

What a good installation quote lists

A written quote should name the tank size, the fuel, the venting and the connectors, and say who handles the permit and the haul-away of the old unit. If a shutoff valve, expansion tank or drain pan is needed, it should be listed as a line item instead of appearing later as an add-on.

Ask how long the visit will take and when the hot water will be back. A clear answer to those questions tells you the plumber has done this job before and planned it before arriving.

Signs the old tank is ready to be replaced

Rust colored hot water

Corrosion inside the tank is shedding into the water.

Rumbling or popping noises

Sediment has hardened on the bottom of the tank.

Water around the base

A leak from the tank itself cannot be repaired.
