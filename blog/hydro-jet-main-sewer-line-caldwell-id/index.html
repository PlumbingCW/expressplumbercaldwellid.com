Hydro Jetting a Main Sewer Line in Caldwell, ID

Hydro jetting cleans a main sewer line with high pressure water, scouring grease, scale and fine roots off the pipe wall. It is the better choice when a cable clears the clog but the line fills up again within weeks.

A camera should come first on old or suspect pipe, because a jet can make a cracked line worse.

What a jet does that a cable does not

A cable bores a hole through a clog, and the pipe wall stays coated. A jet nozzle sprays water forward and backward, and the spray cleans the full circle of the pipe before pushing debris toward the street.

That makes jetting a good match for kitchen lines with grease, older iron pipe with scale and lines where fine roots keep coming back. It is not a repair, though, and a broken pipe will still need one.

Why the camera comes first

A camera shows whether the pipe is whole before high pressure water goes in. A jet in a cracked line can wash soil into the pipe or widen a break, and a camera pass after cleaning shows whether the job worked.

If the camera finds a crack, a sag or a collapse, the next step is sewer line repair, not more cleaning. If the pipe is sound, the jet is the lasting fix.

Boring crews, grease rules and the Caldwell main

In January 2026 a contractor's cross-bore through a city sewer main at Denton Street and Ashton Avenue caused a blockage and a small overflow, according to local news. A cross-bore is a utility line drilled through a sewer pipe, and a jet or cable can cut through it. If boring crews have worked near your home, have the lateral scoped before anyone cleans it.

The City of Caldwell's pretreatment program asks food businesses to use grease interceptors installed by a licensed plumber and to keep cleaning records, and it warns against additives that claim to dissolve grease. The city sewer system includes more than 300 miles of line, and the sewer emergency line is (208) 949-1278.

What a jetting visit costs

Jetting costs more than a cable because the rig is larger and the work takes longer. The price depends on the length of the line, the access and the condition of the pipe, and the plumber gives a price before work starts.

Many homeowners find that a single jet followed by good habits, such as keeping grease and wipes out of drains, stretches the time between visits. Businesses may schedule a visit every few months. See commercial plumbing for kitchens.

Habits that keep the line clear

Scrape pans into the trash instead of the sink, run water for a few seconds after using the disposal and flush only toilet paper. Keep trees with aggressive roots away from the sewer line, and know where your cleanout is.

A camera inspection every few years on an older house is cheap insurance. See sewer camera inspection for what the plumber looks for.
