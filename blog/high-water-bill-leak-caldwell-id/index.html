High Water Bill in Caldwell, ID? How to Find a Hidden Leak

If your water bill jumps and nothing changed in your routine, a leak is the first suspect. Turn off every tap and appliance, read the water meter, wait an hour and read it again. If the number moved, water is escaping on your side of the meter.

A toilet flapper is the most common culprit, followed by irrigation valves and buried lines. This guide walks through each check in the order that finds the leak fastest.

The one hour meter test

1

Turn everything off

Close taps, stop the dishwasher and washer, and make sure no one flushes.

2

Read the meter

Write down the numbers and note whether the small leak dial is moving.

3

Wait an hour

Use no water at all during that time.

4

Read again

Any change in the number means water is leaking somewhere past the meter.

Check the easy suspects first

Put a few drops of food coloring in each toilet tank and wait fifteen minutes without flushing. If color shows in the bowl, the flapper is leaking, and a replacement costs far less than a month of lost water. See toilet repair if you want a plumber to do it.

Then walk the house. Look for a dripping outdoor spigot, a running water softener or humidifier, a water heater relief valve that is dripping and any damp spot on a ceiling, wall or floor.

Irrigation season is a separate story

Some Caldwell homes are on pressurized irrigation run by the Caldwell Municipal Irrigation District rather than city water. Those pumps typically start in mid to late April and shut off in early to mid October, so a lawn leak in summer may not show on the drinking water bill at all.

If your home has an irrigation system on city water, a stuck valve or a cracked line can run all night. Test each zone separately and watch the meter, and if you cannot find it, a plumber can isolate and pressure test each zone as covered on the leak detection page.

How Caldwell treats a leak on your bill

The City of Caldwell's utility billing policy says it will not adjust a bill for excess water from a leak. Narrow exceptions exist, such as a line break or failed parts on the city side of the meter, where water can be adjusted, and sewer charges can be adjusted if the leaked water never reached the sewer.

The city's water ordinance makes the owner responsible for leaks in service pipes and fixtures on the property side, and it says wasteful leaks should be fixed within 60 days of notice from the city. Utility billing is at (208) 455-3000, option 1, and the city's Hearing Board meets on Wednesdays at 9 a.m. for billing appeals.

Winter leaks and frozen pipes

NOAA normals for the nearby Nampa station show roughly 132 days a year at or below freezing. A pipe that splits in a cold snap can leak for hours before anyone notices, and the first sign may be a bill spike weeks later.

If you find a split pipe, close the main valve and call. Burst pipe repair covers what to do in the first minutes, and the plumber will check nearby pipes for damage.

Reading a bill like a detective

Look at the usage graph on the bill, if one is shown, instead of only the total. A steady rise suggests a slow leak, while a sudden jump suggests a break or a stuck valve.

Compare the same month across two years, and note whether you added irrigation, a new appliance or house guests. If the usage doesn't match any change, the meter test is the next step, and the city's utility billing office can confirm your reading at (208) 455-3000, option 1.
