Tankless Gas Water Heater Installation for Caldwell, ID Homes

A tankless gas water heater heats water only when you open a tap, so it never runs out in a long shower. It needs a larger gas line than most tanks, a stainless or plastic vent and a condensate drain on high efficiency models.

Here is what the install involves, what often needs upgrading in a Caldwell house and how rebates are handled.

Why the gas line is the first question

A tankless heater fires at a much higher burn rate than a tank, because it has to heat a flowing stream in seconds. Many houses have a gas line sized for a tank, a furnace and a range, and adding a tankless unit may require a larger line or a new run from the meter.

The plumber adds up the gas load of every appliance and checks the pipe size and length. If the line is too small, flow drops when two appliances run, and the heater can shut down or deliver cold water.

Winter in the Treasure Valley

NOAA normals for the nearby Nampa station show a January average low near 23 degrees F and about 132 days a year at or below freezing. A tankless heater on an exterior wall or in an unheated garage has to be protected from the cold, since the fan, condensate line and exposed pipes can all freeze.

Many models have built-in freeze protection, but it only works with power to the unit. The plumber will choose a location, insulate the lines and explain what to do during a power outage in a freeze.

Caldwell rebates, permits and gas safety

Some Idaho gas utilities offer rebates on high efficiency tankless units. For example, Intermountain Gas Company's residential tankless rebate requires a minimum 0.91 UEF and an application within 90 days of installation. Ask your own gas provider whether a program applies to your address, because we do not name a provider for Caldwell homes.

Idaho says a permit is required when plumbing work is performed, and the City of Caldwell Building Safety Division takes plumbing permit applications online. Gas appliance work is inspected, and the plumber arranges the visit.

Gas and vent work for a tankless unit follows the 2015 Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) with Idaho amendments.

Source: Intermountain Gas Company rebate page, Idaho DOPL Plumbing Bureau FAQ and the City of Caldwell Building Safety Division

Hard water, flushing and upkeep

Minerals form scale inside a tankless heat exchanger, so the unit needs to be flushed about once a year. Published Caldwell water reports do not always list hardness, so test your tap or ask the water department at 208-455-3070 before deciding on a softener.

Isolation valves make the yearly flush a short job. If your water is hard enough to need treatment, see water softener installation.

A realistic picture of install day

A tankless swap usually takes longer than a tank swap because of the gas line, the vent and the wall mounting. Expect the plumber to be on site most of a day, with the water and gas off for part of it.

Once the unit is running, the plumber should walk you through the control panel, the error codes and the flushing valves. Ask for the manual and the model number in writing, since you will need them for warranty and service.

What to do if you smell gas

Leave the building at once. Do not use switches, plugs or a phone inside, then call 911 and your gas utility from outside.

After the utility says it is safe, a licensed plumber can find the leak and repair the connection. For a heater that has failed, see water heater repair.
